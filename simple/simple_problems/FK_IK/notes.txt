
How This Works (Simplified Breakdown)

1. The Setup: The robot has 4 connected segments (2.5, 2.0, 1.5, 1.0 units long). 
The total extension capability is 7.0 units.

2. Forward Kinematics (calculateFK): It uses standard trigonometry (cos and sin). 

Starting from the base (0,0), it loops through each joint, adds up the angles to find the link's global orientation, 
and calculates where the next joint sits.

3. Inverse Kinematics (calculateIK): The CCD algorithm behaves exactly like a human arm trying to touch an object:

	• It looks at the tip of the hand and the ball.
	• It starts at the last joint (the wrist) and rotates it so the hand points directly at the ball.
	• Then it moves back to the elbow, rotating it so the whole lower arm swings closer to the ball.
	• It repeats this backward down to the shoulder until the tip matches the target location.
