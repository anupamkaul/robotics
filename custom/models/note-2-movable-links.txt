Since link2 is currently attached directly to the stationary base (see arm2d_controlled.xml), my XML tree structure is nested flat. 

In a standard humanoid arm configuration, joints must inherit their positions down a hierarchical parent-child chain.

To model a proper shoulder and elbow system, link2 (the elbow/forearm) must be declared inside the XML body block of link1 
(the shoulder/upper arm). 

That way, when the shoulder rotates, the elbow moves with it through space, while still retaining its own independent motor rotation.
(see how arm2d_controlled.xml evolves to arm2d_shoulder_elbow.xml)

---

If only the bottom link is moving and the shoulder stays frozen, it means my d->ctrl assignments are mapped to the wrong actuators or the upper joint cannot overcome internal forces.

To fix this decisively and provide an intuitive environment to study multi-link articulation, let’s expand the system into a 3-joint robotic arm (Shoulder, Elbow, and Wrist). By explicitly defining three cascading parent-child link bodies in the XML and passing three distinct sine waves in C++, we can force the entire body to bend and ripple dynamically through space.
