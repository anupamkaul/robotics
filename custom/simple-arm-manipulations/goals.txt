The Concept Breakdown: Where We Are Headed

I mentioned Inverse Kinematics (IK), and my intuition is absolutely spot on.

Forward Kinematics (Option C): Takes known joint angles (\(\theta_1, \theta_2...\)) and computes exactly where the hand is in 
3D space (\(X, Y, Z\)). We need this first to know if we are touching the target object.

Inverse Kinematics (IK): Does the exact opposite. It takes a desired target coordinate (\(X, Y, Z\)) where an object is placed, and solves for the required joint angles (\(\theta_1, \theta_2...\)) to get the hand there.

The Touch and Grip Loop: Once Forward Kinematics confirms a touch has happened, my Option A Gripper Coordination triggers to pinch and lock onto the object, allowing me to move it to my destination.

Teaching Option A: Mechanical Coordination & Opposing Phases

Right now, my hand joints are executing blind oscillations. To simulate a functional human hand gripping mechanism, 
the fingers must act as an opposing clamp:

The Opposing Target: When the Thumb swings inward (positive angle), Finger 1 and Finger 2 must swing inward from the other side (negative angle) to meet it in the middle.

Phase Lock: They must use the exact same frequency so they open and close at the exact same velocity. 

If they use different frequencies, they will miss each other or clip through one another.

How to Implement This in Your Mind
